What the day held: four runs, about 107 minutes of running — a 30-minute morning recovery jog, then a warmup, the 12×600m, and a cooldown. 21,599 steps, of which 18,507 fall inside the run windows. The load is almost entirely running: 46,268 run SE against 3,092 background. That is the opposite shape from 2026-08-01, where a recovery day breached on 17,513 background steps from a hike — which is why every day is decomposed rather than published as a total.

And it scores 100. The prescription was 1,800 s of recovery plus a warmup, the 12×600m and a cooldown: 109 minutes, which at the measured 175 spm is 19,175 prescribed run steps against 18,507 actually measured — 3.6% apart. The ceiling is 51,775 SE and the day is 49,360. This day is still the strongest check on the whole ceiling formula available anywhere in the repo: an independent prescription and an independent measurement of the same day agreeing to within four percent.

The gap was 1.8% while the ceiling used the population 172 spm, and widened to 3.6% when the baseline supplied this athlete's measured 175. That is not the formula getting worse, and it is worth being explicit about rather than quietly reporting the better-looking number. Prescribed run steps are cadence × prescribed minutes, so a higher cadence raises the estimate; the athlete genuinely runs at ~175, and the prescription genuinely says 109 minutes. What the residual actually measures is that the runs came in a little short of what was prescribed — which is a fact about the day, not about the cadence. The old agreement was closer by coincidence: a 1.7%-low cadence happened to cancel a day run 1.8% under. Two errors cancelling is not an accurate model.

It read 69 until 2026-08-08 — a 45% breach against the flat 34,000 subt entry. That number was not a finding about the athlete, it was the table admitting it could not describe a 110-minute day. subt had one value, and it had to serve a 58-minute 15×1:00 and a 109-minute 12×600m alike. The week of 07-27's Thursday and this Tuesday are both subt and their prescriptions differ by 51 minutes.

And before that, Tuesday read 16,878 SE and also scored 100% — the same score for the opposite reason, which is the whole hazard of a headline percentage. Two separate defects stacked, and each hid the other:

  1. runs.json listed one of the day's four activities. Only the morning jog. So the day's run steps read exactly 172 × 1811 / 60 and the entire evening session was charged to background — the wrong side of the one split this skill exists to compute.
  2. A stale backfill row outranked the live export. garmin-steps.csv carried 6,751 steps for 8/04 — a snapshot taken before the evening session synced — and the old rule was Garmin wins on any date it covers. A day holding 107 minutes of running cannot total 6,751 steps. That figure was published as a measurement, the run was capped to it, and the day reported a background of 0 SE.

The cross-check now states the gap outright:

The backfill undercounted this day by 69%. garmin-steps.csv is a historical backfill assembled by hand on 2026-08-04, not a feed — nothing refreshes it, so past the cutover its rows go stale while outranking an export that is correct. steps_data.health_connect_from is 2026-08-03, and from that date Health Connect supplies the total while Garmin is a cross-check only.

The handover itself verifies. On 2026-08-03, the first day Health Connect owns, both sources read 6,120 — so the switch of authority moved no number where both were sound, and moved a very large one where the backfill had gone stale.
