Rep 1 was run six seconds a mile faster than the top of its band. 6:40/mi against a rep_10min band of 6:46–7:01 from snapshots/pace-charts/2026-08-30.json. The 2:00 jog brought HR from 168 down to 155, a 13-beat drop that cleared the recovery criterion easily — but 155 is a high floor to start a second twelve-minute rep from, and there was nowhere left to go.

This is last week's lesson inverted, precisely. On 8/28 the reps came in 8–18 s/mi slower than the band in 77°F, heart rate read 149–156 against the 162 ceiling, and the session scored 100. That note closed with "there is nothing to chase on the pace side." This Friday was cooler — 75°F / 66% RH against 77°F / 78% — and the reps were run roughly 25 s/mi faster. A sub-T session is scored on heart rate, so chasing the band's fast end is the one thing that can cost the whole session, and it did.

The pace band is a reference here, not the criterion. It is printed because a target the athlete cannot see is a target they cannot aim at — but the number that scores is the heart rate.
