The data behind this week

Monday 10-19 is a real gap and it is permanent — the third Monday in four weeks (09-28, 10-12, 10-19). The watch's rows in Health Connect stop at 09:59 in both exports covering the date (09.19-10.19, 09.20-10.20) while the phone's run to 22:45; a phone-only count undercounts badly and unpredictably, so the day publishes no total. test_parse_steps.KNOWN_GAPS names it.

The Kansas City days are measurements because of a parser fix made the day the week ended. Health Sync renders every record in the phone's timezone at export time, so the exports written in Kansas City on 10-24 and 10-25 put every Local run an hour early and the step pipeline could not find the runs inside their clock windows. parse_steps.align_windows (2026-10-25) snaps each day's run windows to the file's own clock, whole hours, guarded by a running-rate check; 10-23..10-25 went missing → full with it, and no earlier day's total moved.

Sunday's resting HR was corrected 44 → 42 on 2026-11-02, and it moved two numbers. The 10-25 pull was taken mid-day and captured a provisional 44; Runalyze revised it to 42 by the next pull, but parse_wellness kept the first value it met in filename order. It now reads payload files in capture order and takes the later one, on the athlete's ruling. With a resting HR of 42 the 10-TRIMP walking budget converts to fewer steps, so Sunday's ceiling fell 22,558 → 22,209 and the day's score 100 → 99, and the day's TRIMP rose 46.5 → 47.4. Load integrity stays 100 (99.87%). The two sleep cells the correction might have flipped did not move: Runalyze now returns two sleep records for 10-24 and 10-25 — Garmin's and a second API feed whose duration includes awake time and carries no score — and the published cells (7.50 h, no score; 7.07 h / 76) are the records the payload lists first. Both pass the 7.0 h floor either way; which source should win when both exist is an open question for the athlete.
