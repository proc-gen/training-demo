Sunday was unscoreable for a week, and the fix came from the plan

It is the largest day here — 40,584 SE, more than a fifth of the week — and until 2026-08-08 it was excluded from both sides of the ratio, because the sheet's cell ended easy until completion and named no duration. Half a prescription cannot price a day, so it was reported and left out rather than measured against a ceiling nobody set.

The fix was not to infer the missing half. The run itself was about 84 minutes and using that would have been the cardinal error — a ceiling drawn from execution rises to meet the day and scores 100% by construction. The athlete restated the cell as easy until 90 min, and 90 prescribed minutes price out at 43,833 against 40,584 actual. It scores 100 because it was run slightly under what was asked, which is a fact about the day rather than an artefact of the method.

Worth separating from the other reason a Sunday like this can go unscored: on the adherence side it is still reported without a score, because hr.progression_tiers is null and the skill will not grade a progression against the nearest available ceiling. Two different skills, two different questions, and only one of them is now answered.

Monday is scored because the manifest now declares it a rest day. It used to be scored because an unlisted date defaulted to rest, which happened to be right here and is wrong for a manifest written mid-week, where the days that have not happened yet are equally unlisted. Note the stake has gone up: a declared rest day is now priced at the background allowance alone — 2,726 SE, not 8,000 — so mislabelling one is more consequential than it was.

What survives is worth keeping, because the split still says something the total cannot: 30,402 run SE against 6,655 background. Friday was a double — 30 minutes AM and 40 minutes PM, both correctly executed as recovery on the adherence side (80 and 90). It is not a hidden-walking day. Its 6,655 background steps are the second-highest of the week, and stacking them on the week's largest prescribed running volume is what puts it over at all.

That is the opposite shape from 2026-08-01 the following week, where roughly 17,600 of ~30,000 SE was background and the running was 30 treadmill minutes. Same overage, opposite causes, opposite responses — which is why the report decomposes every day rather than publishing a total. Note that the derived ceiling does not blur the distinction, it sharpens it: 08-01 still scores 51 because its prescription was 30 minutes and it walked a hike on top, while Friday scores 93 because it did roughly what it was told.

Two re-derivations have now been run against this day and neither moved the conclusion the same way:

  • 2026-08-06, measured cadence. Friday's run figure moved 29,920 → 30,402 when it stopped being 172 spm × duration, a 1.6% change. Nothing moved. The finding rested on the run/background ratio, 82:18 either way.

The difference between those two is instructive. The first changed a measurement slightly and left the conclusion alone, which is the reassuring outcome. The second changed the standard the measurement was judged against, and that is where a wrong number does its real damage — the load was always 37,058 SE, and every version of this note has said so.

Monday's 866 steps

866 steps for a whole rest day is not plausible as a measurement of a person. It is marked full because Garmin supplied it, and this date precedes the Health Connect cutover so Garmin wins outright here — but the likeliest reading is that the watch was off for most of a day with no run to prompt wearing it.

It does not affect the score: the day is under its 2,726 SE rest ceiling either way. That margin used to be 8,000 and is now a third of it, which is worth noticing — a rest day is priced at the athlete's typical background and nothing else, so an implausibly low reading has less room to hide than it did. 866 steps still clears comfortably; 3,000 would not have.

It does drag the week's background mean down and inflate the day-to-day coefficient of variation, which feeds load-monotony. Flagging it rather than correcting it — there is no better source, and inventing a number would be worse than an implausible one that is honestly labelled.
